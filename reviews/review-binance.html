Binance Review: Unmatched Liquidity, Best-in-Class Tools

ABy Aakash · Updated Oct 2026

Binance is the largest crypto exchange by volume in the world. Here's what makes it worth considering, and where to stay cautious.

What is Binance?

Binance offers spot trading, futures, staking, savings and hundreds of digital assets with the deepest liquidity in the market. Founded in 2017, it grew rapidly to become the world's largest exchange by trading volume, and today supports well over 350 cryptocurrencies and thousands of trading pairs. For active traders, the tight spreads and fast execution make a real difference, especially on high-volume pairs.

Binance also runs its own blockchain (BNB Chain), a native token (BNB) that unlocks fee discounts, and a huge suite of adjacent products — launchpad token sales, NFT marketplace, copy trading, and more — that go well beyond a simple exchange.

Binance fees explained

Binance's standard spot trading fee is a flat 0.1% for both maker and taker orders — simpler than Kraken's tiered system, though less rewarding for high-volume makers. Fees drop further if you hold BNB to pay for trading fees or qualify for VIP volume tiers.

Crypto deposits are free; fiat deposit/withdrawal fees vary by payment provider and region. Network withdrawal fees for crypto are passed through at close to the real on-chain cost, and Binance frequently adjusts these based on network congestion.

Security & regulation

Binance maintains a SAFU (Secure Asset Fund for Users) insurance fund, funded by a portion of trading fees, intended to cover user losses in extreme events. It also publishes periodic proof-of-reserves. That said, Binance has faced significant regulatory scrutiny and settlements in multiple jurisdictions over the years — worth factoring in if regulatory certainty matters to you more than raw liquidity.

How to sign up for Binance

  1. Register — sign up with email or phone number; using a referral link can unlock fee discounts or bonus promotions.
  2. Complete identity verification — standard KYC with ID and a selfie check; basic verification is usually near-instant, with higher tiers taking longer.
  3. Enable 2FA — set up an authenticator app and withdrawal whitelist before funding your account.
  4. Deposit funds — via bank transfer, card, or by depositing crypto directly from another wallet.
  5. Choose your trading mode — start with "Lite" mode if you're new, or go straight to the full Binance trading interface for advanced order types.

Pros & Cons

Pros

  • Largest liquidity and trading volume globally
  • Staking and passive yield products built in
  • Fee discount available with referral sign-up
  • Huge range of supported assets and trading pairs
  • SAFU insurance fund and periodic proof-of-reserves

Cons

  • Regulatory scrutiny varies heavily by region
  • Interface can overwhelm first-time users
  • Not available or fully featured in every country
  • History of regulatory settlements in some jurisdictions

Who it's for

Binance is best suited for active traders who want the deepest liquidity and the widest selection of assets, and who are comfortable navigating a feature-dense platform. If you want a simpler, more conservative option with a longer unblemished security record, Kraken may suit you better.

Frequently asked questions

Is Binance safe and legal to use?

Binance is the largest crypto exchange by volume and operates under varying licenses depending on your region. Availability and regulatory compliance differ by country, so check local rules before signing up.

What are Binance's trading fees?

Binance's standard spot trading fee is 0.1% maker/taker, with further discounts available for holding BNB or using a referral link.

Is Binance good for beginners?

Binance's interface can feel dense for first-time users due to the sheer number of products. Its simplified "Lite" mode is a gentler starting point before moving to the full trading interface.

What is Binance's SAFU fund?

SAFU (Secure Asset Fund for Users) is an insurance fund Binance set aside from a portion of trading fees, intended to cover user losses in extreme security events. It's not a government-backed guarantee, but an added layer of protection.

Can I use Binance in the US?

US residents use a separate, more limited platform called Binance.US rather than the global Binance.com exchange, due to US regulatory requirements. Feature and asset availability differs between the two.
